Labor Economics · Economics of Crime · Public Economics · Financial Economics

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

I use quasi-experimental methods and administrative data to study how criminal justice institutions and public policies shape individual behavior and economic outcomes. My research spans labor economics, economics of crime, public economics, and financial economics.

My job market paper uses novel individual-level administrative data from Florida's Eighth Judicial Circuit to estimate the causal effects of adult pre-arrest diversion. I examine how access to an alternative to traditional criminal case processing affects subsequent criminal justice outcomes. My current projects study diversion policy and fees, social safety-net policy and crime, and labor-market regulation.
