I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Auburn University. My research focuses on the economics of crime, labor economics, and public economics.

My job market paper studies the effects of pre-arrest diversion, examining how access to an alternative to traditional criminal case processing affects subsequent criminal justice outcomes.

More broadly, my research examines how criminal justice institutions and public policies shape individual behavior and economic outcomes. My current projects study diversion policy, social safety-net policy and crime, and labor-market regulation.
